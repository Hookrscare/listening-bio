# Listening.bio 30-Day Acoustic Biodiversity Pilot

## Purpose

Evaluate whether low-cost acoustic monitoring can add useful, auditable evidence to an existing urban ecology, stewardship, research, or education program. The pilot is a methods validation exercise, not a comprehensive biodiversity survey.

## Proposed scope

- Duration: 30 days
- Sites: 3 to 5 locations approved by the site partner
- Sampling: 50 to 100 WAV recordings collected under a repeatable schedule
- Analysis: configured BirdNET candidate detection with model and processing provenance
- Review: human review of a representative, pre-agreed detection sample
- Outputs: CSV, GeoJSON, reviewed detection summary, error analysis, and pilot report

## Study design to agree with partners

- Monitoring question and intended decision use
- Site-selection rationale and required permissions
- Recording duration, time of day, frequency, device, and placement
- Weather, habitat, and recording-effort metadata
- Reference annotation method and reviewer qualifications
- Confidence thresholds and representative review sample
- Privacy, data retention, licensing, and publication rules

## Evidence standard

Listening.bio preserves the source recording, time window, model identity and version, processing configuration, candidate label, confidence, and human-review decision. Automated detections remain candidates until reviewed. Results will distinguish raw candidates, reviewed detections, rejected detections, and unresolved records.

## Roles

**Site partner:** approves locations and collection activity, defines the operational question, and reviews site-specific interpretation.

**Scientific collaborator:** shapes the protocol, annotation method, error analysis, and limits of interpretation.

**Listening.bio:** provides the software, processing workflow, provenance controls, review interface, exports, and pilot report. No software fee is proposed for the initial pilot.

## Success criteria

- At least 90% of planned recordings contain complete source and effort metadata.
- Every reported detection remains traceable to its source audio and processing context.
- A representative detection subset receives documented human review.
- Performance is reported with errors and unresolved cases, not only positive examples.
- Partners determine whether the evidence is useful enough to justify a larger study.

## Proposed first step

A 20-minute scoping call to identify the partner's monitoring question, required permissions, scientific-review capacity, and appropriate pilot dates.
