# AwesomeNYC Application: Listening.bio Public Biodiversity Listening Pilot

Application: https://www.awesomefoundation.org/en/submissions/new?chapter=nyc

Status: Submitted successfully on July 22, 2026. The Awesome Foundation confirmation email states that the application will be considered at the New York City chapter's next deliberation meeting.

## Project title

Listening.bio: A Public Biodiversity Listening Pilot for New York City

## Tell us about your awesome project

New Yorkers share their city with hundreds of bird species, but most people experience that biodiversity without a way to see how it changes across place and time. Listening.bio will create a small public acoustic-biodiversity demonstration using environmental audio from three New York City green-space locations.

We will collect 30 short WAV recordings using a repeatable, privacy-conscious protocol; process them with BirdNET; and invite students, birders, or community volunteers to review the candidate species detections. The result will be a simple public map and an openly shared pilot report showing what was recorded, what the model suggested, what people confirmed or rejected, and what the technology still gets wrong.

The software already works: it can upload real audio, run BirdNET, preserve source and model provenance, map monitoring sites, support human review, and export data. A real public-audio test successfully detected American Robin and also preserved lower-confidence false candidates, demonstrating why community and expert review are essential.

This is not a startup marketing campaign or a claim that AI replaces ecologists. It is a small public experiment in listening to urban nature more carefully and making environmental technology understandable. The project will leave behind a reusable recording protocol, a reviewed dataset, and a short guide that other NYC schools or community groups can try.

## How will you use the money?

$420 for two entry-level field audio recorders and weather protection; $180 for memory cards, batteries, and field supplies; $200 for local travel and three recording sessions; $100 for a community review session; and $100 for accessible map/report materials. No funds will be used for advertising, salaries, or general startup expenses.

## Tell us a little about yourself

I am Rodrigo Castro, the independent developer of Listening.bio. I have built the working audio-upload, BirdNET-processing, evidence-review, mapping, and export workflow. I am now looking to move the project from a technical prototype into a transparent community pilot shaped by ecologists, students, and green-space partners.

## How will this make the community more awesome?

The pilot gives New Yorkers a concrete way to discover, question, and document the wildlife around them. It makes biodiversity monitoring participatory while teaching an important lesson: AI can help people notice nature, but credible evidence still requires context, transparency, and human judgment. The resulting protocol and report will be freely available for local educators and community groups.
