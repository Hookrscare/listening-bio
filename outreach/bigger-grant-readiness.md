# Listening.bio Bigger-Grant Readiness

Updated: July 12, 2026

## Current Position

Listening.bio is ready for micro-grants, partner conversations, accelerator applications, and a small pilot proposal. It is not yet ready to submit a strong $100,000+ scientific or federal proposal as a standalone applicant.

## Critical Missing Requirements

### 1. Eligible legal applicant

Choose one path:

- Form a U.S. company for SBIR/STTR and commercial innovation programs.
- Obtain a 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor for foundation and community grants.
- Have a university, nonprofit, land trust, or public agency lead the application with Listening.bio as the technology partner.

For federal grants, the lead generally needs SAM.gov and Grants.gov registration. Registration can take time and should begin well before a deadline.

### 2. Signed partner commitment

Obtain at least one letter of interest from a park, university, conservation nonprofit, or land trust. The letter should name the pilot location, the partner's role, why the evidence matters, and who will use the results.

### 3. Scientific lead and validation protocol

Name a qualified ecological reviewer. Finalize sampling duration, time of day, site selection, seasonal coverage, confidence thresholds, annotation method, negative controls, and error metrics before collecting field data.

### 4. Real field pilot

Minimum credible evidence package:

- Three to five permitted sites.
- At least 50 to 100 real recordings.
- Complete date, time, location, device, and weather metadata.
- A representative expert-reviewed annotation sample.
- Precision/error results by confidence band.
- CSV, GeoJSON, protocol, and limitations report.

### 5. Licensing and commercialization clearance

Document the permitted use of every public audio file and model. BirdNET's software and model assets may have different terms, and noncommercial restrictions can affect a commercial grant story. Obtain legal review or written permission before promising commercial deployment.

### 6. Technical novelty

For NSF or USDA SBIR, “a dashboard around BirdNET” is not enough. Define and test a defensible innovation such as uncertainty calibration for urban soundscapes, reviewer-efficient active learning, cross-model evidence reconciliation, edge processing, privacy-preserving audio handling, or a validated ecological decision-support method.

### 7. Customer and beneficiary evidence

Complete at least 10 structured interviews with park managers, university researchers, land trusts, environmental educators, or NGOs. Record the current workflow, cost, decision being made, evidence standard, purchasing authority, and willingness to pilot or pay.

### 8. Budget and operating capacity

Prepare legal name, banking, insurance, indirect-cost policy, key-person resumes, project-management plan, data-management plan, safeguarding/privacy policy, and a budget supported by quotes and labor assumptions.

## Fastest 60-Day Path

1. Send the three partnership requests.
2. Submit the AwesomeNYC application.
3. Secure one scientific advisor and one site partner.
4. Finalize the 30-day protocol and permissions.
5. Collect and process 50 to 100 field recordings.
6. Review a representative sample and publish an error report.
7. Obtain two letters of support.
8. Choose the legal applicant/fiscal-sponsor path.
9. Prepare a partner-led $10,000 to $25,000 application.
10. Use the resulting pilot as preliminary evidence for a $100,000+ proposal.

## Larger Targets After These Gaps Close

- Cornell Land Trust Bird Conservation Initiative with an eligible land-trust partner.

- NAWCA for a genuine wetland and migratory-bird conservation project with required match.
- NSF or USDA SBIR after incorporation, customer discovery, and proof of technical novelty.
- State environmental or community-science grants with an eligible New York nonprofit or municipal lead.
